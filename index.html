Tide Coefficient Prediction using Random Forests

A machine learning project using celestial data collected from "ephemerides" and recorded daily tide coefficients to predict tide coefficients using random forest methods.

View on GitHub Explore Results

Project Overview

This project explores the use of Random Forest regression to model and predict tide coefficients based on astronomical features such as angular and radial positions of the sun and the moon and discarding local features depending on the specificity of the city considered, here Brest and Le Havre. This was a first experience with machine-learning methods, coding the classifier from scratch in the context of a project for the competitve exames of Engineer schools where the them was oceans.

Data treatment

The raw dataset consists of the celestial positions of the Moon and the Sun: \(\theta_S, \phi_S, R_{ST}, \theta_L, \phi_L, R_{LT}\), where \(R\) denotes the distance between the Earth and the corresponding celestial object, \(\theta\) is the ecliptic longitude, and \(\phi\) is the ecliptic latitude.

From these raw variables, we construct more meaningful features. Since angles are periodic, representing them directly can be problematic.

  • We encode periodicity using trigonometric transformations: \(\cos(\theta_S)\) and \(\cos(\theta_L)\).

Physical intuition suggests that tidal effects depend on the relative alignment of the Moon and the Sun. When both are aligned, their contributions reinforce each other, leading to stronger tides. When they are perpendicular, the effect is weaker.

  • To capture this interaction, we introduce: \(\cos(\theta_S - \theta_L)\).

With the same insights we could use the inverse of the distance squared as this is the term that appears in Netwton's gravitationnal law but because we use decisions tree that only perform comparisons and this function is monotonous it would not affect the predictions.

Concerning the outputs, we dispose of the daily tide coefficients of two french cities: le Have and Brest. Here we will focus on Brest since they depend less on the local geometry of the sea. Still, as depicted in the results we have to shift the coefficient from the celestial positions from about two days because of the ocean's inertia that introduces delay between actual tides and their causes.
